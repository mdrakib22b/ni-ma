Privacy & terms
Privacy
I built Ni'ma for myself because I wanted to track my own money without handing it to anyone. Then I shared it. That's the whole story, and it explains most of what follows.
No analytics and no ads. You don't need an account to use Ni'ma. If you make one so your devices can sync, I still can't read anything you've entered. The details are below.
What stays on your device
Everything you type: balances, transactions, budgets, goals, holdings, categories, settings. It lives in your browser's storage on this device. If you never turn on sync, I don't have a copy of any of it. I couldn't look at your data if you asked me to, and I couldn't lose it, leak it, or be made to hand it over.
What leaves your device
Two things, and you decide on both: the ticker symbols in your portfolio when live prices are on, and an encrypted copy of your data if you turn on sync.
Ni'ma sends the ticker symbols in your portfolio to a small function I run, which asks Finnhub for quotes and the European Central Bank for exchange rates. Like any web request, that carries your IP address.
It sends the symbol. Nothing else. Not your share count, not your cost basis, not your balances, not your name. Anyone watching that traffic would learn a device somewhere is curious about AAPL. They'd learn nothing about your finances.
I don't log those requests. If you'd rather send nothing at all, use Ni'ma offline. Prices freeze at their last known values and everything else works.
Sync, if you turn it on
Settings › Account & sync backs up your data and keeps your phone and computer on the same numbers. Before anything leaves your device, the app encrypts it with AES-256-GCM, using a key that only your password or your recovery key can open.
Your password never reaches me. The app turns it into two keys on your device. One signs you in. The other unlocks your data, and it stays on the device. What the server gets is your email address, the sign-in key, and a block of encrypted data with the time it last changed. I can't read that data, and someone who broke into the server couldn't either.
The server is a Supabase database, and the small functions in front of it run on Vercel, where the app itself is hosted. Like any web request, sync carries your IP address, so those hosts see it the way they'd see any visitor.
Reminders add one more thing. If you turn them on while signed in, the server keeps the dates something is due over the next two months, so it knows which mornings to wake your phone. It gets dates and nothing else: no amounts, no names. Apple or Google delivers the notification, and all it says is that a bill is due.
Forgot your password? Reset it by email. Your data comes back as long as you still have a signed-in device or your recovery key. If you have neither, nobody can open the synced copy, including me. That's frustrating when it happens to you, and it's also exactly why the copy is safe.
Encryption on this device
Settings › App passcode encrypts everything stored on this device with AES-256-GCM. The key comes from your passcode and isn't saved anywhere. There's no reset. Forget it and the copy on this device is gone, and I can't get it back for you. If you sync, you can erase the app, sign back in, and pick up where you left off.
Deleting everything
Settings › Erase all data wipes this device, and so does clearing the site data or deleting the app. If you made an account, Settings › Account & sync › Delete account removes it and your synced copy from the server for good. Signing out deletes nothing. It just stops this device from syncing.

Terms
Ni'ma is a side project. I made it for my own use and put it out in case it's useful to someone else. It's free, and it will stay free, because there's no billing system and no data to sell.
What it isn't
Not a bank, not a broker, not an advisor. Nothing here is financial or tax advice. Prices and FX rates come from third parties and may be delayed or wrong. Check anything that matters.
Your data is genuinely yours, including the risk
Without sync, your data lives on your device and nowhere else. A cleared browser, a lost phone, a forgotten passcode or an uninstall takes it with it, and I can't restore what I never had. Turn on sync, or download a backup every so often. It's the one thing I'd actually ask you to do.
Accounts
An account is optional. It exists for sync and reminders and nothing else. Keeping your password and recovery key safe is on you. I can help you reset a password, but I can't open your data, so I can't recover it either.
If sync goes down
Sync runs on modest hosting that I keep an eye on, but I can't promise it will always be up, or that every reminder will arrive. If I ever have to switch sync off for good, I'll say so in the app first. Whatever is on your devices stays there either way.
No warranty
I wrote this alone, on evenings and weekends. It has bugs I haven't found. It might get a number wrong. Use it at your own risk and don't make it the only record of anything important.
Liability
To the extent the law allows, I'm not liable for losses from using Ni'ma, including lost data, a reminder that didn't show up, or decisions made on numbers it showed you.
